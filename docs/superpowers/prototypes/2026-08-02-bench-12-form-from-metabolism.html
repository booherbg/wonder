Form from metabolism

Wonder already draws fourteen plant forms, by hand, and they are the best-looking thing in the game. Bench 0 throws all fourteen away and grows every plant from one grammar, which is why its shelf comes out samey. This bench tests the third option: keep the drawers, and let the chain choose which one runs and what it is set to. The fourteen drawers below are imported from src/render/plantSprites.ts unmodified — this is the art the game ships today.

The system

Three ways to decide what a plant looks like, compared on one page. Wonder today picks a form from a habitat table and never changes it, then drifts nine numeric traits. Bench 0 has no forms at all: it replays the metabolic chain as drawing instructions, one grammar for everything. The proposal here is that the chain should not draw the plant — it should choose the drawer and set the traits, leaving the fourteen hand-made forms in place and making them earned rather than assigned.

Parts

form
One of Wonder's fourteen plant archetypes — flower, shrub, tree, fungus, fern, coral, succulent, reed, vine, grass, moss, bulb, sporestalk, kelp. Each is a hand-written pixel drawer on a 16 × 28 sprite. In the shipping game, form is picked from the tile type at birth and never mutates.
trait
The nine numbers that vary within a form: hue, accent hue, saturation, height, spread, petals, leaves, lean, glow. These do drift, generation to generation.
habitat table
Which forms each tile type allows — kelp only in shallow water, succulents on sand. This bench keeps it, because it is what stops the island reading as arbitrary.
chain features
Four numbers in [0,1] summarising what a metabolism is like, computed from the run: climb how far up the tier ladder it got, branch the share of steps that were upgrades or trades, dependence the share needing a catalyst, and thrift its yield over cost. These are the whole interface between chemistry and appearance.
prototype
A point in that four-dimensional feature space assigned to each form — what kind of metabolism that shape means. A tree is a long branching expensive chain; moss is a chain that barely climbs; a fungus is one that cannot run without a catalyst somebody else supplies.
variety
Measured two ways, so "they all look the same" stops being an opinion. Distinct forms counts silhouettes actually used. Mean pairwise difference is the fraction of pixels that differ between two sprites, averaged over every pair on the shelf.

Procedure

  1. Roll an island chemistry and sixteen plants from it — bench 0's model, imported unchanged.
  2. Run each plant's chain and derive its pigment, exactly as bench 0 does.
  3. Render it bench 0's way: replay the chain as drawing instructions on a 16 × 28 plate. Measure variety across the shelf.
  4. Compute the four chain features from the same run.
  5. Pick a form: among the forms this habitat allows, take the one whose prototype is nearest the plant's feature vector, with each axis weighted.
  6. Fill the nine traits from the chain's outputs — pigment hue into hue, the top reagent's hue into the accent, chroma into saturation, yield into height, branching into spread, blooms into petals, whorls into leaves, and tier 4 into glow.
  7. Render it Wonder's way, through the real drawer, and measure variety again on the identical set of sixteen chains.

Findings

Pixel variance does not measure "they all look the same", and it nearly fooled me

I built this expecting the left shelf to score badly and it does not. Measured as silhouette difference — Jaccard distance between ink masks, colour ignored — bench 0's grammar scores 65%, and the proposed version scores 65–75% depending on habitat, a mean ratio of 1.09×. By that number there is almost no improvement, and the first draft of this section claimed a 3× gain that the instrument then refused to produce.

The number is right and the metric is wrong. Bench 0's plants do differ pixel by pixel — they differ in size, in step count, in how far they lean. What they do not differ in is kind. Every one is a branching stick with blobs on it. Wonder's forms differ categorically: a mushroom is not a small tree, a moss cushion is not a short reed. So the measure that actually tracks the complaint is the count of distinct kinds on the shelf — 1 for bench 0, by construction, against a mean of 6.0 and a maximum of 10 for the proposal. That is the finding, and the pixel metric is left on the page because a metric that disagrees with your eyes is worth seeing.

The obvious version of the mapping collapses

Matching a chain's features against a universal scale — the design anyone would write first — puts nearly every plant on the same prototype. Across four habitats and eight seeds it used a mean of 2.9 silhouettes, and silhouette variance actually fell to 0.84× of bench 0's. The cause is that chains on one island are metabolically similar: they draw from the same deck, so their branch and thrift numbers cluster in one corner of the space and the nearest prototype is the same corner for everybody.

Ranking each feature within the island's own population fixes it: 2.9 silhouettes becomes 6.0, and shape variance goes from 0.84× to 1.09×. The island grades on a curve. This is not a tuning detail — it is the difference between the mechanism working and not, it is one function, and I would not have found it by reasoning. Toggle matching in the rail and watch the right shelf collapse.

Form is the third channel of legible causation, and it is nearly free

Wonder rolls form from habitat and never changes it, so a shape means nothing except "this tile allows it". Under this proposal terrain still constrains — kelp stays in the water — but which allowed form you get reads back to how the plant makes its living. A chain that cannot fire without somebody else's catalyst lands on fungus or coral. A long branching chain that pays for itself lands on tree. One that barely climbs lands on moss. The fourteen drawers keep doing exactly what they do now; they just acquire a reason.

It also answers the thing bench 0 got wrong. Bench 0 replaced the drawers because the chain "should" produce the body. It should not: it should select the body. Wonder's fourteen forms are hand-made and good, and a procedural grammar that reaches the same quality is a much larger bet than it looked.

Essence is currently a punishment

Section 4 computes it live and the shape does not depend on the seed. The energy ladder is exponential — 1, 3, 9, 27 — while the cost ladder is factorial, because each rung spends t+1 units of the rung below: 1, 2, 6, 24 base units. Factorial always overtakes exponential. In energy returned per base unit spent the rungs go 1.00 → 1.50 → 1.50 → 1.13. Climbing pays until compound and then stops. A fifth tier would return 0.68.

Across 8000 plants on 400 rolled worlds only 3.6% ever end holding essence. Those that do show a better yield (2.46 against 2.07 for base-only), but that is selection on the hand rather than a payoff from the tier — they are the plants whose recipes happened to chain well, and they would have scored well one rung lower too. What essence genuinely buys is colour: pigment per base spent keeps climbing (7.50 against compound's 6.67) and mean chroma goes 0.139 → 0.206.

So the jewel tones are currently earned by being inefficient, which under bench 6's selection means the most beautiful plants are the ones selection removes. Two fixes, and I would take both.

  • Stop the penalty. The intended rule is visibly energy = 1.5 × what it cost you: it holds exactly at tiers 1, 2 and 3 and breaks only at 4. Setting essence to 36 energy instead of 27 restores it — one constant.
  • Pay essence in something other than calories. Let it buy the rarest form, the bulb — Wonder's hanging lantern — plus the glow trait and pollinator preference. That is how expensive secondary chemistry works in real plants: it does not feed you, it makes something else come to you. And it makes the answer to "is there a benefit to being essence" something you can see, which is the point of the whole project.

What this does not settle

The prototype table is my assignment, not a measurement: fourteen points placed by argument about what each shape ought to mean metabolically. It is the obvious thing to disagree with, which is why it is a live table on the page rather than buried in the code. Nothing here tests whether a player can read form back to cause — that needs a person, and it is the same untested claim the whole suite rests on. And the drawers stay hand-made under this proposal: the chain chooses among fourteen, it does not invent a fifteenth. Genuinely new shapes need bench 3's attractor morphology, and that is a different and much larger bet.
