The reagent economy

A world rolls its own chemistry. A plant inherits a small hand of conversions and runs it once, at maturity. What comes out is energy, a pigment, and a body — all three from the same computation. Nothing here is decorative: every colour on this page below the rule was earned by a chain that actually fired.

Parameters

These are the dials the whole model hangs off. Every one of them re-rolls the chemistry and the flora, so you are changing the world rather than restyling it. The defaults are the values I tuned to; the interesting thing to do is break them.

How to read this

Colour is a readout, not a decoration. Hue is the circular mean of what the plant ended up holding, weighted steeply by tier — so the rarest thing in the pile is what colours it. Chroma reads the pigment-weighted mean tier rather than the ceiling, because one stray unit of essence in a heap of base shouldn't blaze like a plant that is mostly essence. That makes saturation a measure of metabolic sophistication: tier-one scrapers come out sage and ochre, and the jewel tones have to be earned outright. Lightness is yield over cost, so a plant in the wrong soil visibly pales.

The body is the same computation. There is no separate morphology genome. Each conversion emits one growth instruction as it fires — a gather extends, an upgrade branches, a trade puts out a whorl, a catalysed step swells a node — and the roots are the same program run downward. A plant that runs an elaborate chain grows an elaborate body, always, because they are the same thing. Form cannot lie about function here.

Catalysts are the reason to care about your neighbours. A catalyst has to be present, not spent. Cards that need one are marked in the log. That single rule turns a field of rivals into a field of relationships: whatever first leaks a catalyst onto a tile opens a branch of chemistry for everything that grows there afterwards.

Worth poking at: roll worlds until you find one with a narrow hue spread — those come out tonal and harmonious. Wide-spread worlds go polychrome. Then look for a plant that reached tier four; it should be the only thing on the shelf you can't stop looking at. If that isn't true, the chroma ladder needs work, and that's exactly the kind of thing this bench is for.
