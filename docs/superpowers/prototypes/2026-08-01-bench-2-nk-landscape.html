The ruggedness dial

Wonder has no fitness function. Reproduction is a flat constant and death is a coin flip, so what looks like evolution is drift — and drift, as the bottom panel shows, sits at fitness 0.500 forever no matter how long you watch. Before writing a real one we need to know how rugged to make it. Kauffman's NK model is the instrument for exactly that: one integer, K, moves a landscape from a single glassy peak to pure noise. Everything below is measured live from the seed.

The system

An NK landscape is a fitness function you can dial from smooth to random with one integer. A genome is N binary loci; each locus contributes a fitness value that depends on its own bit and on K others. Fitness is the mean of the N contributions. At K=0 every locus is independent and the landscape has a single peak you can climb one bit at a time; at K=N−1 every contribution depends on everything, and the landscape is white noise. Everything else on this page is measured from that.

Parts

genome
N bits. The whole search space is 2N genomes; at N=12 that is 4096, so the landscape can be enumerated exactly rather than sampled.
K
How many other loci each locus's contribution depends on — its epistatic partners. This is the ruggedness dial and the only structural parameter.
contribution
A uniform value in [0,1] drawn deterministically by hashing (locus, its bit, its partners' bits, seed). Because it is a hash rather than a stored table, the landscape needs no memory and is reproducible from the seed alone.
fitness
The mean of the N contributions. Always in [0,1], and a random genome sits at 0.500 — which is why a drifting population parks there forever.
local optimum
A genome no single bit-flip improves. Counting them is the direct measure of ruggedness; a menu of many good peaks is a different design outcome from one best answer.
adaptive walk
From a random start, repeatedly flip the bit that improves fitness (greedy) or any bit that improves it (random ascent), until stuck. Walk length is how many steps adaptation gets before it runs out of moves.
correlation length
How far along a random walk fitness stays autocorrelated. Long means the landscape gives usable gradient information; short means neighbours tell you nothing.
selection strength
The exponent on fitness in the reproduction weight. 0 is Wonder's current model — reproduction ignores fitness — and it is the control the bottom panel exists to show.

Procedure

  1. Build the landscape from (N, K, model, seed): for each locus, pick K partners and define its contribution as a hash of the relevant bits.
  2. Enumerate or sample the space to find every local optimum and the basin each one drains.
  3. Run adaptive walks from random starts and record their length and endpoint.
  4. Measure autocorrelation along a random walk to get the correlation length.
  5. Sweep K from 0 to N−1, re-measuring reachable fitness at each — the chart that answers the design question.
  6. Sweep N at fixed K to test whether the complexity catastrophe bites at the genome sizes Wonder actually uses.
  7. Run a live population: pop individuals, reproduction weighted by fitnesssel, per-locus mutation μ, for a fixed number of generations.

4 · The complexity catastrophe

Kauffman's most uncomfortable result: hold K at the maximum and grow the genome, and the fitness a lineage can actually reach sinks back toward 0.500 — toward chance. A genome that gets too big stops being able to optimise at all. Hold K at a small constant instead and the decline never starts. Shaded band is the genome size Wonder actually uses.

How to read this

K is how many other genes each gene cares about. A locus with K = 0 contributes the same amount to fitness no matter what the rest of the genome is doing; a locus with K = 5 contributes a value that depends on its own state and the state of five others, so improving it can silently ruin them. That is the whole model. N is the number of loci, K is the entanglement between them, and every number on this page falls out of those two.

K = 0 · Fujiyama
One peak, and every lineage finds it. The landscape is a single smooth cone: each locus has one better state, they never conflict, and hill-climbing is just fixing them one at a time. Correlation length is longest here (7.6 mutations at N = 16) and walks are longest (8 steps) — but every walk ends in the same place. Perfect convergence, zero variety, and — the part people forget — lower fitness than every K from 1 to 9: 0.680, against 0.724 at K = 3. Only the top of the range (K ≥ 10) does worse. A landscape with no interactions has no happy accidents in it either.
K = N−1 · fully random
Every genotype's fitness is an independent draw. Neighbouring genotypes tell you nothing about each other, correlation length is 0.11, and there are 3833 local optima in a space of 65536 — one peak every seventeen genotypes. Walks die after 1.5 steps. Nothing adapts; lineages just freeze wherever they landed. This is noise wearing a genome.
2 ≤ K ≤ 4 · correlated rugged
Many peaks, but the peaks are arranged — nearby genotypes still resemble each other, so climbing works and yet there is more than one answer. This band contains the highest fitness any lineage actually attains, at every N we tested. It is the only regime where "adapting" and "having options" are simultaneously true.

The recommendation: K = 3

Set Wonder's epistasis to three partners per trait, with a workable band of K = 2–4. Four measurements agree on it, and none of them is a matter of taste:

  • It is the fitness maximum, and that is not obvious. Mean fitness reached at N = 16 runs 0.680 (K = 0) → 0.716 (K = 2) → 0.724 (K = 3) → 0.710 (K = 4) → 0.705 (K = 6) → 0.652 (K = 15). Sweeping the argmax across N = 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 gives K = 4, 3, 2, 3, 3, 3. The best K is a small constant, not a fraction of the genome — so this recommendation survives Wonder's genome growing.
  • It produces a menu, not a monolith and not a haystack. At N = 16, K = 3 there are 58 local optima in 65536 genotypes. The single most popular one captures 12.5% of independent lineages, the top three capture 29%. At K = 0 the top peak captures 100% (one answer, always); at K = 6 it captures 3.7% and at K = 15, 0.8% (no answer is ever "the" answer).
  • Adaptation stays legible. Correlation length at K = 3 is 3.3 mutations, so a mutation's effect predicts its neighbourhood for about three more mutations — long enough that a lineage visibly trends. Walks take 4.9 steps, so adapting is a handful of observable events rather than one jump or an endless shuffle.
  • A real population lands where we want it. Population of 200, µ = 0.02, selection 6, 200 generations, N = 16: K = 3 ends at fitness 0.712 sitting on 4.0 distinct peaks, with the dominant peak holding 87% of the population. That is a reading of "one common form plus a few odd rare ones" — which is what a living island should look like. K = 0 gives exactly 1.0 peaks and 100% dominance; K = 15 gives 12.1 peaks but fitness 0.653, i.e. lots of forms, none of them good at anything.

If forced to one sentence: K = 0 is a world with one right answer, K = N−1 is a world with no right answers, and K = 3 is a world with about five.

Two things worth knowing about the instrument

The textbook autocorrelation formula is wrong at low K, and this bench shows it. Weinberger's standard result is ρ(1) = 1 − (K+1)/N. Measured on 30 000-step random walks it is not: at N = 12, K = 0 the measurement is 0.835, not 0.917. The derivation assumes each locus's contribution carries the full variance of a uniform, but a K = 0 locus only ever takes two values, so its within-landscape variance is half that. Correcting for it gives ρ(1) = 1 − (K+1)/(N(1 − 2−(K+1))) = 0.833, which matches to three decimals. The readout shows all three numbers so you can watch the classic form converge onto the corrected one as K rises past about six. Nothing downstream depends on this — but a bench that quietly disagrees with its own theory line is a bench nobody should trust.

Random and adjacent neighbours are not the same, and correlation length cannot tell them apart. Over 24 seeds, adjacent neighbours produce reliably more local optima at the same K — 47.0 ± 8.0 vs 40.0 ± 6.3 at N = 12, K = 4; 218 ± 17 vs 178 ± 15 at N = 14, K = 6 — while correlation length barely moves (1.78 → 1.75, and 1.41 → 1.41, both well inside seed noise). So the single statistic everyone quotes for ruggedness is blind to a real 15–25% difference in the number of places a lineage can get stuck. If Wonder's traits end up laid out with local structure (a stem's traits interacting with the stem's other traits), expect somewhat more peaks than the K number alone would predict.

What this does and does not tell us about Wonder

NK is a null model. It has no structure on purpose. Its dependency graph is flat and arbitrary — locus 7 interacts with locus 2 for no reason and to no pattern. Real genomes, and the genome Wonder is building toward, are modular: root traits interact with root traits, pigment chemistry with pigment chemistry, and the modules barely talk. Modular landscapes are measurably kinder than NK at the same average K, because a lineage can solve one module while another is still wrong. So treat K = 3 as an upper bound on entanglement, not a target to hit. If Wonder's traits turn out to cluster naturally, the same felt ruggedness will arrive at a higher nominal K.

It also tells us nothing about where fitness comes from. NK fitness is a hash — it is arbitrary by construction. Wonder's has to be earned from the world: light, water, pollinators, the reagent chemistry from bench 1. This page is only about the shape of that function, not its content. The one place the two meet is worth flagging: if fitness ends up being mostly "does your chemistry chain fire on this soil", that is already a strongly epistatic function, and its effective K may be well above 3 before anyone has chosen a number. Measure it once it exists; don't assume it.

And the honest null result: at the genome sizes Wonder actually uses, the complexity catastrophe is real but not our problem. At maximum K it costs 0.669 → 0.644 in attained fitness going from 10 traits to 20 — about two and a half points out of a seventeen-point band above chance, a decline you would struggle to notice in play. The curve fits 0.5 + c/√N with c somewhere between 0.5 and 0.7 depending on how far out you fit it, which puts a genuinely catastrophic 0.55 at roughly 100–200 loci. That spread is the honest answer rather than a failure to compute one: the 1/√N form is asymptotic and we are nowhere near the asymptote, so the only precision this claim can carry is an order of magnitude more traits than Wonder will ever have. At fixed low K there is no decline at all across the whole range. Wonder can grow its genome; it just cannot grow K alongside it.

What a player would ever perceive

None of this is visible as a number. Ruggedness surfaces as exactly one thing: whether an island's lineages converge on one obvious solution or stay stuck in several odd ones — and that is a real aesthetic difference, probably the most important one on this page.

At K = 0 an island resolves. Give it long enough and every plant on it is the same plant, because there is one peak and everything finds it; the island is finished, and a finished island is a dead one to look at. At K = 15 nothing ever resolves — every lineage freezes two mutations from where it started, the flora is a permanent uniform mush of half-solutions, and because nothing is better than anything else nothing reads as adapted. Both failure modes look the same from a distance, which is the trap: total convergence and total noise are both visually flat.

At K = 3 the island produces a dominant form plus three or four persistent oddities — the population numbers say 4.0 peaks with the leader at 87%, which reads as one plant you see everywhere and a handful you see in particular corners. Those minority peaks are stable, because they are genuine local optima and not drift, so the player can learn them, name them, go looking for them. And because the modal basin only holds 12.5% of independent lineages, the next island answers the same question differently. That is the property worth buying: not variety within a run, which noise also provides, but variety between runs that still looks purposeful within one.

The one player-visible risk of K = 3 over K = 1: a lineage can get stuck somewhere plainly worse than its neighbours and stay there, which reads as the world being obtuse rather than alive. If playtesting turns that up, the fix is not to lower K — it is to raise the mutation rate, which lets a lineage tunnel off a bad peak without flattening the landscape everyone else is climbing. Try it on the bottom panel: at K = 3, µ ≈ 0.05 keeps the peak count while lifting the mean.
