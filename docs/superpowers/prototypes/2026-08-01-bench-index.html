Eleven benches for a living ecology

Wonder's ecology has no fitness function, frozen fauna, and eleven of fourteen plant forms outside every food web. Rebuilding it means choosing among a lot of plausible mechanisms, so rather than argue, these are instruments: eleven parameterised models, each isolating one mechanism and each answering one question with a number. Every dial is live and every run is seeded, so anything you see here you can get back. Benches 1–7 ask whether the mechanisms work; benches 8–11 ask whether they read, which for this game is the harder half.

Do the mechanisms work?

Do they read?

The first seven benches all measured mechanism. For a game whose first pillar is beauty, that was the wrong half to spend seven benches on. These four are the correction.

8
Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion

Surface pattern

Structure grammars cannot make spots and stripes; reaction–diffusion is what does it in life. But pretty is easy — the question is whether a small genetic change gives a small pattern change, and whether anything survives being shrunk to a sprite.

What it foundHeritable, with one mandatory change: the growth seed must be inherited. Re-roll it and a genetically identical sibling lands further from its parent than an unrelated genome does — one integer is the whole difference. Safe mutation step is δ ≤ 0.008; the cliffs sit in the tail, not the mean, because only 22% of the parameter rectangle makes a pattern at all. Crop the sprite to 2–3 field cells per pixel: a 7×7 map then carries about two features — two dots, one bar, or plain.

9
the question nobody answered with pixels

The host plate

Blaine asked “is 30×50 big enough?” and it has only ever been answered in prose. Five resolutions side by side, insects perched at true scale, and the roots drawn over the soil they are eating.

What it foundYes for the shoot — ship 30×74. Going to 64×104 costs 4.4× the pixels and buys two more flowers; ink coverage actually falls as the plate grows. The insect estimate in the doc was 3× too small (9px, not 2–3), so legibility was never the constraint — crowding is. A plate holds two pollinators, three at a stretch, at any resolution. And insects come out cryptic by default, landing 5.4° of hue from their host, so value contrast rather than colour is what makes them visible.

10
colour at the scene scale

Island palette and light

Twenty individually-justified colours can still compose into mud. Does biasing a world’s hues toward a harmonic key make islands more beautiful without making them all alike — and does earned colour survive being tinted by dusk?

What it foundSplit-complementary, grounded to the terrain, at bias 0.70 — and grounding matters more than the key. Variety improved rather than suffered, because an unbiased island has no character to differ in. But earned colour does not survive dusk: pigment separation falls from 93% to 35%, below half for over half the day. It is the tint, not the darkness, and glow currently fades on luminance when the damage peaks with tint. One-line fix, deliberately left broken so the table still shows it.

11
the phenotype we forgot

Motion signature

At gameplay zoom an insect is about five pixels, so you can barely see its colour or shape. What you can see is how it moves. Is motion legible as a lineage identity, and does it work as a second camouflage axis?

What it found89.1% identification against a 12.5% chance level, from genomes drawn at random. At 5px motion scores 88.9% against colour’s 58.3% — the curves cross at 8.5px, and that direction holds across every swept assumption. Which composes with bench 9: if insects mostly wear their host’s colour anyway, motion is not a nice extra, it is the channel that still works. The feed-versus-be-seen trade-off is a real Pareto front that collapses to a single optimum when the predator is switched off.

Century: Spice Road · OKLCH

The reagent economy

A world rolls its own chemistry; a plant inherits a small hand of conversion cards and runs it once, at maturity. Energy, pigment and body all come out of that same computation — so colour is a readout of metabolism rather than a decoration, and the plant's silhouette cannot lie about what it does for a living. Nine live dials, from hue spread to pigment steepness.

What it found Upgrades must be generic or a random hand is dead on arrival; rung cost must escalate or every plant lands on the same tier; pigment weight has to climb far more steeply than energy or leftover base swamps the colour; and branching must keep the parent tip alive, or every plant renders as the same broom. Try seed 7 or 42 for a polychrome world — 2438 and 5150 come out tonal.

Why these seven

The 2026-07-24 audit found four capabilities structurally missing — selection, niche, origination and disturbance — and a fifth that matters more than any of them: legible causation. A purple flower is decoration; a purple flower you can trace back three steps to a cause is the thing this game keeps reaching for and has never been able to deliver.

The benches divide along that list. Bench 1 is origination, and it is the only mechanism here that can put something genuinely new into a world. Bench 2 asks whether adaptation is possible at all at a given landscape ruggedness — if that answer is wrong nothing built above it will work. Bench 3 asks where form comes from. Bench 4 tests whether Holland's original tag ecology gives us more than the slice we already ship. Bench 5 settles two specific coupling decisions with numbers. Bench 6 is the integration, and bench 7 is the one capability the peaceful pillar seems to forbid.

Two of them are deliberately set up to fail. Bench 7 tests the intermediate disturbance hypothesis rather than assuming it, because that hypothesis is genuinely contested in the literature. Bench 4 tests whether tag ecology's structure survives being made non-lethal, because Wonder cannot ship visible death and a mechanism that only works when things die is no use to us. Negative results from those two would be worth more than positive ones.
