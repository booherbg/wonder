Wonder · ecology · parameter study · 2026-08-01

Twelve benches for a living ecology

Rebuilding Wonder's ecology means choosing among a lot of plausible mechanisms. Rather than argue, these are instruments: twelve parameterised models, each isolating one mechanism and each answering one question with a number. Every dial is live and every run is seeded, so anything you see here you can get back.

Executive summary

The problem. Wonder has no fitness function. Reproduction is a flat constant and death is a coin flip, so what looks like evolution is drift, and drift sits at the population mean forever no matter how long you watch. The 2026-07-24 audit found four capabilities structurally missing — selection, niche, origination and disturbance — and a fifth that matters more than any of them: legible causation. A purple flower is decoration. A purple flower you can trace back three steps to a cause is the thing this game keeps reaching for and has never been able to deliver.

The proposal under test. An island rolls its own chemistry: a set of reagents and a deck of conversion recipes. A plant inherits a small hand of those recipes and runs it once, at maturity, against what its roots drew out of the ground. Energy, colour and body shape are all read off that single computation, so colour is a readout of metabolism rather than a decoration, and a plant's silhouette cannot lie about what it does for a living. Bench 0 is that economy standing alone; every other bench either supplies a mechanism it needs or tests whether it can be seen.

What the twelve divide into. Benches 1–7 ask whether the mechanisms work: origination (1), how rugged to make the fitness landscape (2), where body form comes from (3), what Holland's full tag model buys over the slice we ship (4), how energy should climb the food chain (5), whether the whole thing coheres on a grid (6), and whether disturbance survives being made peaceful (7). Benches 8–11 ask whether any of it reads, which for this game is the harder half: surface pattern (8), how big the host plate has to be (9), colour at scene scale (10), and what is left of a phenotype at five pixels (11).

Decisions these produced

1
Gate worldgen on maxRAF ≥ 5 reactions, not on "a RAF exists" — the naive test fires 3× too early. Measure the transition once for the L you ship rather than guessing a constant.
2
Set landscape ruggedness to K = 3 (band 2–4). It does not scale with genome size, so the genome can grow later without re-tuning.
3
Boolean-network morphology at N = 32, K = 2, p = 0.40: 70% of mutations silent, and effect sizes bimodal — the punctuated jump the game cannot currently produce.
4
Tag ecology gives trophic layers and an arms race, but not from death — lethal and non-lethal runs came out bit-identical. Ship the quiet mode, not full rates-only.
5
Couple trophic levels by ceiling, not funding: top-level persistence 1.000 vs 0.490 over 200 paired seeds. Periodic forcing buys nothing; build the seed bank instead.
6
Selection visibly beats drift on a grid — mean tier 2.31 vs 1.07 — from two lines of code. Half of rolled worlds are duds, so the rejection sampler is not optional.
7
Disturbance does produce a diversity hump, and the peaceful version beats the lethal one (4.91 vs 4.52). Wonder can have this capability without shipping visible death.
8
Reaction–diffusion markings are heritable only if the growth seed is inherited. Safe mutation step δ ≤ 0.008.
9
Ship a 30 × 74 host plate. Bigger buys margins, not detail — and a plate holds two pollinators at any resolution, because the limit is crowding.
10
Bias island hues to a split-complementary key grounded to the terrain, at 0.70. Variety improves rather than suffering. Earned colour does not survive dusk without a fix.
11
Motion beats colour below 8.5 px — 88.9% vs 58.3% at Wonder's normal zoom. Combined with bench 9's crypsis result, motion is the channel that still works.

The benches, in order

Every one opens standalone, carries its own definitions and procedure at the top, and states its own findings at the bottom. Nothing here is wired to the game.

Two of them are set up to fail

Bench 7 tests the intermediate disturbance hypothesis rather than assuming it, because that hypothesis is genuinely contested — the hump appears in roughly 16% of published studies, not in most of them. Bench 4 tests whether tag ecology's structure survives being made non-lethal, because Wonder cannot ship visible death and a mechanism that only works when things die is no use to us. Negative results from those two would have been worth more than positive ones, and bench 4's headline result is in fact a negative: it was never death doing the work.
