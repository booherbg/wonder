Thirteen benches for a living ecology

A bench is a small working model of one mechanism, with every dial exposed. None of these is the game. Each one exists to settle a single design question by measuring it, before anything gets built on the answer.

What this is for

The problem, in what a player sees

Wonder's plants change from one generation to the next, but nothing decides which changes are worth keeping. A plant with a body that suits its ground has exactly the same chance of having offspring as one that does not. So an island wanders instead of adapting: watch it for an hour and it is different, but it is not better suited to anywhere. Nothing new ever appears that was not in the starting deck, nothing is ever cleared away to make room, and eleven of the fourteen plant forms have no relationship with any animal at all.

Underneath that there is one missing piece with a name: a fitness function — any rule that lets the world tell a good plant from a bad one. Without it, change is random walk. With it, change accumulates in a direction, and a direction is something a person can notice and follow.

What we actually want

Something happens on your island that you did not expect, and you can find out why in about three steps. That is the whole target, and it is stricter than it sounds: a purple flower is decoration, but a purple flower you can trace back to the mineral seam its roots reached is a story. Everything below is in service of the second thing.

The idea being tested

Give each island its own chemistry. The ground holds a handful of raw substances; the island also rolls a set of recipes for converting cheap substances into rarer ones. A plant inherits three to five of those recipes — that is its whole genome — and runs them once when it matures, using whatever its roots pulled up. What it ends up holding determines three things at once: how much energy it earned, what colour it is, and what shape it grew. One computation, three visible consequences. That is what makes a plant's appearance evidence rather than decoration, and it is what bench 0 is.

What the other eleven are doing

The economy needs mechanisms it does not itself supply, and it has to survive being looked at. So: 1–7 ask whether the mechanisms work — where new chemistry comes from, how much difficulty a world should have, where body shape comes from, what animals do to each other, how energy moves up a food chain, whether the whole thing holds together on a map, and whether a peaceful game can still have weather that clears ground. 8–11 ask whether any of it can be seen at the size Wonder actually draws: markings, how big a plant portrait needs to be, whether twenty earned colours compose or turn to mud, and what survives when an insect is five pixels across.

Start with 0 if you want the core idea. Otherwise pick whichever question you care about — every bench stands alone and defines its own terms at the top.

The words that keep coming up

Borrowed from biology, and used precisely below. Each has a plain meaning here.

fitness
Any number the world uses to rank one individual against another. Wonder has none today, which is the root problem.
drift
Change with no ranking behind it. Things vary, nothing improves. This is Wonder's current behaviour, and it is the control every bench compares against.
selection
The opposite: individuals that score better leave more offspring. Two lines of code, and it is the difference between an island that wanders and one that adapts.
niche
A way to make a living that is different from your neighbour's. Without distinct niches, one strategy wins everywhere and the island goes monotone.
origination
Something genuinely new appearing that was not in the starting set. Rearranging what already exists is not the same thing, and Wonder currently only rearranges.
disturbance
Anything that clears ground — fire, flood, storm. It sounds violent, and the open question was whether a peaceful game can get the benefit without it.
reagent
One substance in an island's chemistry. Has a rank from 1 (common, cheap) to 4 (rare, valuable) and a fixed colour.
chain
A plant running its inherited recipes in order until nothing more can fire. The thing it ends up holding is what its colour, energy and shape are read from.
legibility
Whether a player can actually perceive a mechanism. A mechanism nobody can see is not a feature, which is why four of the twelve benches test only this.

What the benches decided

Each line is a decision plus the measurement behind it. The bench it came from carries the full working.

0
Make recipe upgrades generic — spend any two of a rank for one of the next — or a randomly dealt hand cannot chain at all and the plant is born dead.
1
Throw away a rolled chemistry unless at least five of its reactions can sustain each other. Testing whether merely one can fires 3× too early and passes dead islands.
2
Let each gene's effect depend on about three others. Fewer and every island solves itself the same way; more and nothing can adapt at all.
3
Build bodies so that 70% of mutations change nothing visible and the rest jump. That mix is what gives a species a stable look it can still break out of.
4
Animals form food chains and arms races on their own, and — the useful surprise — death was never what produced them. The peaceful version ran bit-for-bit identical.
5
Let each level of the food chain cap the one above it rather than pay for it. Paying collapses the top level in half of all runs; capping never does.
6
Selection visibly beats drift on a real map, from two lines of code — plants reach rank 2.31 vs 1.07. Half of all rolled worlds are duds, so worlds must be re-rolled until one passes.
7
Clearing ground does raise diversity, and the peaceful version beat the violent one. Wonder can have weather that makes room without anything visibly dying.
8
Markings pass from parent to child only if one extra number is inherited too — the seed the pattern grew from. Miss it and siblings look like strangers.
9
Draw plant portraits at 30 × 74 pixels. Bigger buys margins, not detail. And a portrait fits two insects at any size, so crowding is the limit, not resolution.
10
Pull every island's colours toward a shared key anchored to its own dirt. Islands got more distinct from each other, not less. But the colours wash out at dusk, unfixed.
11
At the size Wonder draws insects, how a thing moves is more recognisable than what colour it is — 89% vs 58%. Motion is the identity channel that survives the zoom.
12
Let a plant's chemistry choose one of Wonder's fourteen existing plant shapes rather than replace them — distinct silhouettes go from 1 to 6. Rank a chain against the others on its own island, not against a universal scale, or it collapses back to 3.

The benches, in order

Every one opens standalone, carries its own definitions and procedure at the top, and states its own findings at the bottom. Nothing here is wired to the game.

Two of them are set up to fail

Bench 7 tests the intermediate disturbance hypothesis rather than assuming it, because that hypothesis is genuinely contested — the hump appears in roughly 16% of published studies, not in most of them. Bench 4 tests whether tag ecology's structure survives being made non-lethal, because Wonder cannot ship visible death and a mechanism that only works when things die is no use to us. Negative results from those two would have been worth more than positive ones, and bench 4's headline result is in fact a negative: it was never death doing the work.
