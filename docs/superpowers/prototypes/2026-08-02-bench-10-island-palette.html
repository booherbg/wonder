The island palette

A plant's colour is earned: hue from the reagents its metabolism ended up holding, chroma from how sophisticated that metabolism is, lightness from how well it is doing. That works on a specimen plate. This bench asks the question the plate cannot — what do twenty of them look like standing in the same field, on real ground, under real light? Every colour below the rule was computed, including the dirt.

The system

Twenty plants standing in one field. Each one's colour is earned — hue from the reagents its metabolism ended up holding, chroma from how sophisticated that metabolism is, lightness from how well it is doing — which is a system with no opinion whatsoever about what twenty of them look like together. This bench adds one step between the earned hue and the drawn hue: pull it toward a harmonic key. Then it measures whether that helps, whether it costs variety, and whether any of it survives being tinted by the light.

Parts

key
A set of hue anchors on the colour wheel: analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triad, tetrad, or none. Six of them, comparable side by side.
root mode
Where the anchors are placed. grounded pins the first anchor to the island's terrain hue, so the flora and the dirt are in the same key. free lets the key float. This choice turns out to matter more than which key you pick.
bias
How hard an earned hue is pulled toward its nearest anchor. 0 leaves the roll untouched; 1 squeezes every hue into a narrow arc around an anchor. The pull is applied to the output hue, so the metabolism is unchanged — this is a display transform, not a change to the chemistry.
chroma ceiling
The most saturated a fully earned pigment may be. Everything below scales off it, so this is the dial that decides whether a tier-4 plant is a jewel or merely a brighter leaf.
lightness spread
How far yield-over-cost is allowed to push lightness either side of mid. At 0 the island flattens and every plant reads as the same health.
glow
A trait carried by some fraction of species. At night it is the only channel still carrying pigment information, which is why its share is a dial.
light model
Time of day produces a multiply-and-tint applied to every colour in the scene, plus depth haze toward the horizon. Both are applied in OKLCH so hue survives, but the tint is exactly what erodes the differences.
separation
Mean perceptual distance between species colours as actually drawn. This is the number that says whether earned colour is still doing its job — it is measured after the light, not before.
adherence
How close the island's hues sit to their anchors. High adherence with low variety is just monochrome, so the two are always read together.

Procedure

  1. Roll an island: a chemistry, a terrain hue, and species distinct metabolisms, each running its chain to earn a pigment.
  2. Place the anchors for the current key, rooted to the terrain or free.
  3. Pull each earned hue toward its nearest anchor by the bias, and cap chroma and lightness with the ceiling and spread.
  4. Lay out the scene — ground, horizon, scattered plants at the current density — and render it under the light model at the current hour.
  5. Measure separation, adherence, collision count and variety, then sweep every key × bias combination over many islands to fill the comparison table.
  6. Scrub the hour from dawn to dusk, re-measuring separation at each step: the light survival table is that sweep.

Does the bias cost us variety?

The two numbers pull in opposite directions and that tension is the whole finding. Island difference is how unlike two islands' palettes look side by side (12-bin circular hue histogram, chroma-weighted, total-variation distance — rotation sensitive). Structure repeat is the same distance computed on the histogram of pairwise hue intervals, which is rotation invariant: it asks whether every island has the same shape even when it has a different root hue. High island difference is good. High structure repeat means samey.

How to read this

A harmony key is a bias, not a constraint. Each key is a set of anchor hues on a rolled root — one anchor for analogous, two opposed for complementary, three at 120° for triadic, and so on. A reagent still rolls a uniform hue; the bias then compresses its distance from the nearest anchor. At bias 0 nothing moves and you are looking at Wonder as it ships today. At bias 1 a hue is squeezed into a narrow arc around its anchor (±37° for analogous, ±12° for tetradic), but which anchor it lands on is still the roll's choice. The key never picks the colours; it only decides which colours are near each other.

The terrain participates. Sand, grass, forest, marsh, rock, water and sky are pulled toward the same anchors at 40% of the flora's bias, which is enough to make the ground belong to the chord and not enough to stop sand being sand. Every palette statistic on this page includes those seven colours. A palette measured only over the plants is measuring a specimen plate again.

The key root control is the one that matters most. Rolled, each island picks its root hue freely and the terrain is merely dragged toward whatever chord came up. Grounded, the root is chosen so that one anchor lands exactly on the terrain green — the ground is a member of the chord rather than a guest. That single switch turns out to be worth more than the choice of key, which is the finding I did not expect and the reason the table below re-runs when you flip it.

Plants do not inherit the key directly. This matters more than I expected. A plant's hue is the tier-weighted circular mean of the reagents in its final pile, so a plant holding two reagents from two different anchors lands between them — in the interval the key was trying to keep empty. The key is applied to the chemistry; the flora only sees it through the metabolism. The "chord adherence" readout is exactly this leak, measured: mean degrees from each species hue to its nearest anchor.

Light is applied in OKLab, not as an overlay. Time of day produces a luminance factor, a chroma factor and a tint colour; each rendered colour is scaled and then mixed toward the tint. That is why the survival table can be honest — the same transform that draws the pixels produces the numbers, so there is no way for the picture and the measurement to disagree.

Findings

Written after running it, and two of these came out against what I expected. Sweep figures are 14 islands per configuration, 10 species each, seeds 1000 + 7919·i, with chroma ceiling 0.16 and lightness spread 0.40. The light figures are averaged over 5 island seeds, 45 species pairs each. The control everywhere is no key at all, which is Wonder as it ships today: scene discord 26.1%, flora discord 23.5%, island difference 0.302, character spread 10.4°.

A key that ignores the ground buys almost nothing

This is the result that reorganised the rest. With the root rolled, biasing the reagents to a chord does exactly what you would hope to the flora — complementary at bias 1.0 takes flora discord from 23.5% down to 8.3% — and yet the whole-scene number barely moves, 26.1% → 23.4%. The plants agree with each other and go on disagreeing with the dirt. Switch the root to grounded, which puts one anchor on the terrain green and changes nothing else, and the same key drops the scene to 20.3%; grounded tetradic reaches 9.6%, a 63% reduction against control. On the default island, six of the fifteen colours in the discord count are ground or sky, and they carry 69% of the weight in the tonality figure. Harmony that stops at the flora is measuring a specimen plate again.

Recommendation: split-complementary, grounded root, bias 0.70

At those settings: scene discord 19.5% (−25% against control), flora discord 18.8%, island difference 0.380 (up 26%), character spread 11.2° (up 8%). It is the only configuration in the sweep where every number moves the right way at once.

Grounded tetradic and grounded triadic score better on beauty — 9.6% and 11.1% at bias 1.0 — and I am not recommending them, for a reason the metrics cannot see. Their offsets are closed under their own rotation, so grounding them produces one identical anchor set for every island in the game: tetradic grounded is always {62°, 152°, 242°, 332°}. The island-difference number stays flat (0.288) because islands still differ in which anchors they populate, which is exactly the blind spot: the statistic cannot tell "different palette" from "different proportions of the same four paints". Split-complementary's offsets (0/150/210) are not rotation-symmetric, so grounding it yields three distinct chords rather than one.

Bias 0.70 rather than 1.0 is also a judgement call the numbers do not force — grounded split at 1.0 is strictly better on discord (17.3%) with the same variety figures. The argument is arithmetic about arc width. At bias b a hue at the chord boundary lands 60(1−b) + 14b degrees from its anchor, so the reachable band is ±28° at 0.70 and ±14° at 1.0. Across the three grounded split chords that is five anchor hues, which means 78% of the hue wheel remains reachable at 0.70 and 39% at 1.0. A game whose flora can only occupy 39% of the wheel has stopped having a palette and started having a brand guideline. I would go to 0.8 and no further.

Variety does not measurably suffer — it improves

This was the risk and the answer is genuinely the opposite of what I expected. Island difference rises under a rolled root — 0.302 with no key, 0.514 for split at bias 0.70, 0.607 for complementary at 1.0. The reason is that an unbiased island has no character to differ in: ten uniformly-rolled reagent hues make a flat hue histogram and every flat histogram resembles every other one. Give an island a chord and a rolled root and it acquires a colour it can be unlike the next island in.

The exception, and the trap, is analogous. It is the only key that makes measured discord worse — 26.1% → 31.3% rolled at 0.70, → 37.7% grounded — because squeezing the flora into one arc does nothing about the ground, and the arc tends to land at a mid-distance from it, which is precisely the discord band. Grounded analogous then collapses variety outright: island difference 0.302 → 0.141, character spread 10.4° → 2.1°. It is the key that looks best in a single screenshot and worst in a game. If you take one number from this bench, take that one.

The chord leaks, and that is a feature

Chord adherence is measured twice on purpose. For grounded split-complementary at bias 1.0 the reagents average 7.7° from their nearest anchor while the flora that eat them average 15.1° — the leak roughly doubles the distance. At the recommended 0.70 it is 15.3° against 21.2°, and on the default island the live readout shows 12° against 26°. The leak is the circular mean in the pigment step: a plant holding reagents from two different anchors is genuinely an in-between colour, and it should be. That is what stops a triadic island reading as three paint pots. The mechanism that makes colour meaningful is also the mechanism that softens the key — a pleasant accident rather than a designed one, and a reason not to reach for bias 1.0.

Earned colour does not survive dusk. Glow is the fix, and it fires too late

Measured against unlit pigment separation, averaged over five islands: full daylight (08:00–16:30) retains 93% with 4 of 45 species pairs indistinct. The dusk twilight peak at 17:48 retains 35% with 22.8 of 45 indistinct; deep twilight at 18:18 retains 24% with 35 of 45; night sits at 27% with 30 of 45. Mean hue rotation at the twilight peaks is 60–62° — more than a sixth of the wheel, applied to everything at once. Retention is below 50% from roughly 17:30 to 06:30, which is 54% of the modelled cycle. Stated plainly: for more than half the day, a plant's pigment is not communicating what its metabolism did. Almost all of that is the tint term rather than the darkness — at the twilight peak the model mixes 49% of the way to a single warm colour, and mixing everything toward one colour is definitionally the destruction of a colour code.

The good news is that Wonder already ships the fix. A glowing plant is mixed back toward its own pigment instead of toward the tint, and that restores night separation to 108% of unlit — better than unlit, because glow also lifts chroma 25% — with indistinct pairs down from 30 to 3.6 of 45. So the design implication is concrete and slightly uncomfortable: glow is not a cosmetic trait, it is the night-time readability channel, and a 10% glow share means 90% of the island goes colour-blind at sunset.

The second implication is a one-line bug. This bench fades glow in on luminance, so at the twilight peak — where the tint is strongest and the damage is worst — glow has barely started, and it recovers only 74% there against 108% at full night. The trough is not night, it is dusk. Driving the glow weight off the tint term instead of the luminance term would close it, and costs nothing. I have deliberately not made that change here, so the table still shows the problem.

Depth haze compounds all of this and is the cheapest thing on the page to get wrong. At the default 0.45, a plant standing at the horizon retains 51% of its separation against 93% at the viewer's feet; at 0.75 it is 23%, and at 1.0 the far bands are literally one colour. Haze buys scene coherence very cheaply, which is exactly why it is tempting to overdo. I would cap it at 0.35.

What a player would actually see

Three things, none of which require them to know why. First, an island reads as a place with a colour before it reads as individual plants — compare the control cell in the strip, where coral, olive and violet turn up in the same square metre of grass, against the split cell where the same twenty metabolisms land on a coral-and-sage chord that the sand and the sea are also in. Second, the next island is still a different place, because the root rolls; the measured island difference is higher with the key than without it. Third — and this is the one they would complain about without being able to name — at dusk the island stops having colours and starts having one colour, and if the glowing plants are rare they will not notice the flora again until morning.

What this does not tell us

The discord metric is a heuristic. The 25–65° band is a working stand-in for "reads as an accident", drawn from ordinary colour practice rather than from a perceptual result; it is directionally trustworthy and it is not a measurement of beauty. The strip is the actual evidence for the beauty half and it is a picture, which is the honest status of that claim. The bench also fixes the terrain palette by hand — a Wonder island whose ground varies by biome would need the 40% terrain pull re-tuned — and it models none of silhouette, foliage density or texture, all of which plausibly do more for scene coherence than hue does. And the light model is one artistic choice among many: a gentler golden hour would produce a gentler finding, so the number to take from the dusk section is not "35%" but "the tint fraction is the parameter that decides whether your colour code survives, and nobody had picked it on purpose".
