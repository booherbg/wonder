Part I

The field guide — what the island actually does

Every number below was read from source at commit 300ce6a, not from design intent. Where a measured figure is quoted (web density, chain counts, seed tiers) it comes from the 14-seed and 2,500-seed harness runs recorded in docs/superpowers/research/food-web-and-byproducts.md, 2026-07-19.

1 · The layer stackwhat is actually running

Seven systems run on the island. They are far more independent than they look. The critical structural fact: there are two unrelated genome systems — plants use nine HSL-ish floats, insects use a 49-cell discrete color grid — and they touch through exactly one scalar.

The one bridge between the two genomes

flowerSizeFor(sp) maps a plant species' petals (3–10) to the number of "jackpot" accent cells in its 7×7 flower map. That single integer is the entire causal channel from plant genetics to insect adaptation. The flower map's actual colors are drawn from a hash of the species id — they are not derived from the plant's hue at all. A red flower and a blue flower of the same species have identical insect-facing signatures.

Species generation, per island

  • Six habitats (Grass, Forest, Sand, ShallowWater, Rock, Marsh) each roll 3–5 species → 18–30 base species.
  • Forest slot 0 is forced to Tree — every island has at least one true tree.
  • Exactly one sport (✶) per island: height ×1.6, sat = 1, glow 0.85–1.0, petals +3.
  • 15% of islands roll a crater → 2 endemics (⟡) confined to a homeland radius, sat = 1, glow ≥ 0.75.
  • Substrate-feeder flags are rolled last off a separate salted stream (seed ^ 0x5b1e).
  • Up to 12 daughter species (✧) can arise during play — see §12.
Habitats are hard walls

Deep water, Snow, Scree, Highland and Cliff host zero plant species — HABITAT_FORMS lists only six tiles. Roughly a third of a mountainous island's surface is sterile by construction. rootableAt refuses every off-habitat placement for every sim route; the only waiver in the codebase is the player's own sow onto a tilled tile.

Six of nine drifting traits are decorative

height, spread, leaves, lean, hue2 and sat are read only by plantSprites.ts, speciesName, and phenoKey (the sprite cache key). Nothing in the sim consults them. Consequently they drift as pure Brownian noise — they have no selective landscape at all, and driftDistance (which averages all nine equally) is dominated by them. Speciation is therefore triggered mostly by cosmetic drift.

The three genome operations

// one parent, one generation of drift mutate(g, rng, amount=0.05): for each trait t: g[t] += (rng()*2-1) * amount * span(t) // uniform, not gaussian // two same-species parents within pollinationRadius cross(a, b, rng, amount=0.04): mid = (a[t] + b[t]) / 2 // hues take the shorter arc out[t] = mid + (rng()*2-1) * amount * span(t) // normalized genome distance, 0 = identical driftDistance(a, b) = sqrt( Σ (Δt / span(t))² / 9 ) // hue distance doubled after wrap

Live mutation amount is 0.06 (DEFAULT_TUNING.mutationAmount), ×1.25 while raining. Crossing uses that same value, not the 0.04 default.

Trees are outside the insect economy entirely

Every island is guaranteed a tree, forests are majority-tree by density (0.7 + rng()*0.3 vs 0.3 + rng()*0.7), and no tree can ever host a swarm, be pollinated by one, or benefit from insect spread. Forest canopy is a demographic island. The same is true for the entire aquatic flora except shallow-water Flowers and Shrubs — Kelp and Coral have no animal interaction of any kind (they are also excluded from critter palates at fauna.ts:454).

Which of an island's species can actually host insects

Counting from HABITAT_FORMS weights: Flowers appear in 5 of 6 habitat pools, Shrubs in all 6, Succulents in 2. A typical 24-species island lands around 7–10 flowering species, of which the swarm layer will only ever work the handful nearest its spawn points. Seed 42's ledger (below) shows 7 swarms working 3 of the island's flowering kinds.

6 · How pollination actually workstwo different mechanisms share one word

There are two things called pollination and they are unrelated. Only one of them mixes genes.

A · Flora self-cross genetic

Inside simTick. When a mature plant reseeds, it looks for a same-species neighbour within pollinationRadius = 2 tiles. If one exists, the child is cross(parent, partner); if not, it is mutate(parent). No animal is involved. Applies to all 14 forms equally — trees, kelp, moss included.

B · Swarm pollination not genetic

Inside SwarmLayer.tick. A well-matched cloud calls flora.pollinateSpread(host, 6, 2) — which drops a drifted clone of the host up to 6 tiles away. It calls mutate, never cross. No pollen moves between individuals. Mechanically this is accelerated wide dispersal, not pollination.

The insects do not pollinate, in the genetic sense

pollinateSpread (flora.ts:496) is byte-for-byte propagate with a wider radius and a per-tile same-species cap. Both call mutate(p.genome, …). Nothing in the swarm layer ever combines two plants' genomes. A flower worked by a devoted cloud for a thousand ticks is genetically no more mixed than one nobody visits — it is simply more numerous and more spread out.

The swarm pollination probability chain

// per swarm, per sim tick — game/swarms.ts:809-826 gate 1: match = metabolicEfficiency(sensor, flowerMap, accent) if match < 0.30 → no pollination at all gate 2: fill = population / cap P = 0.5 × match² × fill gate 3: island-wide ceiling of 3 pollinations per tick gate 4: addPlant must find an open, correct-habitat tile with <2 of this species // worked example: match 0.6, cloud at 60% fill P = 0.5 × 0.36 × 0.6 = 0.108 per tick → ~1 spread every 9 ticks (18 s) // a perfect cloud at full fill P = 0.5 × 1.0 × 1.0 = 0.50 per tick → ~1 spread every 4 s, until the ceiling bites

With 8 swarms, the 3-per-tick island ceiling is reached whenever roughly three clouds sit above match ≈ 0.7. At 3 spreads/tick that is 90 new plants per minute from the insect layer alone — comfortably the fastest propagation route in the game when clouds are well-adapted.

7 · The insect genomea 7×7 grid of 7 states — the best mechanic in the codebase

Plants and insects meet in a shared tag space: a 49-cell grid where each cell holds 0 (neutral) or one of 6 colors. A plant presents a flower map; an insect carries a sensor map that evolves toward it. Adaptation is literally visible — the insect's drawn colors are its genome.

life/idmap.ts:24–27 (weights) · :70 matchReward · :88 metabolicEfficiency · :95 resemblance

Selection inside a cloud

// life/swarm.ts:66 — every sim tick, per swarm evolveSwarm(swarm, flower, rng): sort the 10 maps in the pool by matchReward, descending keep the top 5 refill to 10 by mutateMap(random survivor, 3 cell flips) sensor = pool[0] // the representative body drawn on screen

Truncation selection at 50%, 3 flips per offspring, every 2 seconds. This is an extremely fast optimizer — a naive cloud typically reaches match ≈ 0.5 within a few dozen ticks (~2 minutes), which is why WARM_TICKS = 20 is enough to make a loading island's swarms already colored toward their flowers.

The gene pool is bookkeeping, not bodies

A swarm has 10 IdMaps and one float population (0–96). The 42 drawn Motes are pure animation state with no genetics. "Population 458" in the ledger is a sum of floats across clouds. Nothing is ever born or dies as a discrete event — by design ("peaceful — population rises and falls, nothing dies as an event").

8 · Swarm dynamicsthe population equation and its equilibrium

// life/swarm.ts:86-117 — one tick, in order 1 regenNectar flower.nectar = min(1, nectar + 0.05) 2 feedSwarm drawn = min(nectar, 0.25) gain = drawn × efficiency × 4 × (0.6 + 0.4·nerve) energy = min(1, energy + gain) 3 evolveSwarm truncation selection, see §7 4 updatePopulation energy −= 0.02 // LIVING_COST target = energy × cap pop += (target − pop) × 0.05 // 5% lerp toward target 5 applyPredation (only if pressure > 0; live islands use 0.6) exposure = 0.4 + 0.6·nerve loss = pop × (1 − resemblance) × pressure × 0.02 × exposure

Equilibrium arithmetic

At steady state a cloud draws only what regenerates: 0.05/tick. So

gain = 0.05 × eff × 4 × boldness = 0.2 × eff × boldness cost = 0.02 break-even efficiency = 0.02 / (0.2 × boldness) ≈ 0.10 … 0.17

A cloud below roughly 12% metabolic match loses energy every tick and shrinks toward zero population; above it, it grows toward energy × 96. Since a random starting sensor typically scores 0.05–0.15, a naive cloud is right on the survival knife-edge and must adapt within its first few dozen ticks — which it reliably does, because truncation selection at 3 flips/offspring is very fast. The tension is real but always resolves the same way.

Predation

WORLD_PREDATION = 0.6 is a constant standing in for "generic insectivores." There is no insectivore entity. Loss scales with 1 − resemblance, so a cloud that looks like its flower is spared. Peak drain at full exposure and zero resemblance is pop × 0.6 × 0.02 × 1.0 = 1.2% per tick — meaningful but never wiping.

Divergence → cousins

  • Attempted every 50 ticks (~100 s), at most one bud per attempt island-wide.
  • Requires the pool to be genuinely bimodal: ≥2 maps favouring the home flower and ≥2 favouring a different nearby flowering species. No forced splits.
  • Child takes 40% of the population, mutated behavior (±0.15), and homes on the other species. Marked ✧.
  • Hard ceiling SWARM_COUNT_CAP = 24.

9 · Critters5–8 kinds, ~30 bodies, frozen for the life of the island

Critters have no population dynamics whatsoever

spawnCritters runs once at world load and produces 4–6 individuals per kind. Nothing anywhere adds to or removes from that array during play — verified by grep across main.ts and fauna.ts. No birth, no death, no starvation (HUNGER_CAP = 0.95 exists precisely so that hunger can never outrank the drive to sleep). Palate, role, morph and den are all fixed at generation. Fauna do not evolve. The only evolving layers on the island are plants and insect clouds.

The palate — one function gates every fauna→flora interaction

// life/fauna.ts:194 appetite(palate, genome): if genome.form ≠ palate.form → 0 // hard form gate hueDist = wrapped |genome.hue − palate.hueCenter| hueScore = max(0, 1 − hueDist / palate.hueWidth) glowScore = 1 − |genome.glow − (palate.glowTaste+1)/2| return hueScore × (0.6 + 0.4 × glowScore) APPETITE_MIN = 0.30 // below this a plant is just scenery

A palate is cut from one plant species at generation: hueCenter = arch.hue ± 0.03, hueWidth = 0.12 + rng·0.14, glowTaste = arch.glow·2 − 1 ± 0.25. So a critter is born loving one kind and generalises outward by color. Measured across 14 seeds: ~1.6 plant species per critter, and 63% of an island's flora is eaten by nobody.

Drives — deterministic, no dice in motive

hunger = 0.95 × clamp01((0.85 − energy) / (0.85 − 0.35)) // FULL, HUNGRY comfort = max(darkness, clamp01((0.10 − energy) / 0.05)) // SPENT·2 curiosity = accumulator, +0.12/s beside a still wanderer, −0.2/s otherwise, cap 0.55 dominant = strongest above 0.20; ties fall to hunger → comfort → curiosity

Energy drains at 1/200 per second (empty in ~3.3 min awake), refills 1/60/s asleep. Because comfort saturates to 1 below SPENT = 0.05 and hunger caps at 0.95, an exhausted critter always chooses the den over food. That is the "nothing starves" invariant, enforced by two constants.

Bodies

8 body plans × legs × 6 tails × 7 crowns × 3 eye counts × 5 coat patterns, all hashed deterministically from four numbers (bodyHue, earLen, tailLen, size) via morphOf. Sizes are dealt from bands so every island has one tiny scurrier and one knee-high ambler. Body plan is rerolled up to 12× to avoid duplicates within an island. Entirely cosmetic — nothing below the skin reads morphology.

The effective per-plant birth rate is much lower than 0.06

simBudget = 480 plants are examined per tick, sampled with replacement from all. At a typical population of 8,000, any given plant is examined on 6% of ticks. Effective reproduction ≈ 0.06 × 480/8000 = 0.0036 per plant per tick — one child every ~280 ticks ≈ 9.3 minutes of wall clock, or about 1.3 island days. The island-wide birth rate is what's actually fixed: at most 480 × 0.06 ≈ 29 births/tick, independent of population size. This makes flora growth linear, not exponential, and it is the real reason the island feels stable.

11 · Every way a plant leavesthe three brakes

RouteRateGate
crowding thin P = (N/10000 − 0.8) × 0.6 species count > 12 · not tended · target chosen uniformly at random

There is no selection anywhere in flora mortality

The crowding thin picks a plant at random from the sampled 480. Old age is genome-blind. Grazing is the only genome-sensitive removal — and it selects against being palatable, which is the opposite of what dispersal selects for. Net: a plant's genome has essentially no effect on its expected number of descendants. What the game calls evolution is, mechanically, a random walk with a speciation tripwire.

At default settings the island opens at 8,000 plants (life = 80%) against a comfort threshold of 8,000 — so crowding thinning is active from tick 1, at P ≈ 0 and rising. At the 10,000 cap it reaches P = 0.12 per examination, removing ~58 plants/tick against ~29 births/tick. The system is strongly buffered around 8,000–9,000.

12 · Speciationthe founder-cluster rule

Checked on every newborn plant. All five gates must pass:

1 tick − lastSplitTick ≥ 500 // island-wide cooldown, ~17 min 2 driftDistance(child, species.archetype) ≥ 0.30 3 daughters so far < 12 4 kin within 4 tiles, same species, driftDistance(kin, child) ≤ 0.14 5 kin count (incl. the child) ≥ 6 // on success: the whole kin cluster crosses over to the new species id

The daughter inherits the parent's habitat and density, takes a fresh coined word, keeps the family epithet, and wears ✧. Its archetype is the child's genome. It is appended to the shared species list, so the swarm layer will lazily build it a flower map if it can bloom (flowerFor) — meaning a daughter species can acquire its own pollinator cloud.

Why splits are rare, and cosmetic when they happen

driftDistance averages all nine traits equally, and six of them are decorative (§4). A cluster therefore crosses the 0.30 threshold mostly through drift in lean, leaves, spread and hue2 — traits with no ecological meaning. Combined with the 500-tick cooldown and the 12-daughter cap, an island produces at most 12 splits ever, most of them ecologically identical to their parent. ?split=1 loosens this to cooldown 30 / distance 0.18 / cluster 4 for testing.

Chain potential is scored at generation time, with no simulation: diversityScore = chains + 2·(redundancy − 1), and new random islands are rejection-sampled to a floor of 5 across 8 candidates. Named tiers: flat (≤1) · sparse (<5) · living (<15) · rich (<30) · lush (<40) · legendary (≥40). Only 0.6% of seeds are legendary; seed 2438 "Polpol Skerry" scores 71 chains with 69 closing.

Chains are hue-blind to habitat at scoring time but not at runtime

chainStats (the generation heuristic) ignores habitat; the actual stepSubstrates germination additionally requires the feeder's habitat to equal the tile the substrate landed on. Real chain formation is therefore strictly rarer than the score suggests — a disperser that eats a Grass-habitat plant and walks onto Sand leaves a byproduct no Grass feeder can use.

14 · Beast, birds, ambient lifewhat else moves, and what it does

The beast ecological

70% of islands. One per island, no den. Palate cut like a critter's. Picks up a burr (P = 0.08 when a favored plant is within 1.5 tiles, cargo cap 2), walks 32 tiles, then sows a drifted child at its feet. Speed 26 px/s. The only long-distance gene flow in the game — everything else is ≤6 tiles. Forages purposefully 75% of the time toward distant stands of what it favors.

Birds decorative

1–2 flocks of 3–6. Fly → settle → perch → flush when you come within 2.5 tiles. Roost through the night. They eat nothing and affect nothing. A ready-made insectivore chassis sitting unused next to a hard-coded WORLD_PREDATION = 0.6 constant.

Fish · frogs · dragonflies decorative

Viewport-scoped: spawned when fewer than N are on screen, filtered out the moment they leave. Wall-clock dt, private un-seeded RNGs, no persistence, no sim state. Dragonflies vanish entirely above darkness 0.45. Pure atmosphere.

Foreground parallax decorative

Seed-fluff on two virtual planes sliding at 1.35× and 1.75× camera speed, positions hashed from plane cells. Zero allocation, zero state.

Part II

The expansion audit — what the ecology cannot yet express

Every claim about current behaviour below is sourced in Part I. This half is about closing the gap between the machinery Wonder has built and the ecology it can actually show — not by adding systems, but by making the ones that exist consequential. A markdown copy lives at docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-24-ecology-expansion-audit.md for git review.

1 · The diagnosisone paragraph

Wonder has built an extraordinary amount of machinery for evolution and almost no evolution. Plants carry nine heritable traits, cross, mutate, speciate, and log their history — but their reproduction rate is a flat constant, their mortality is uniformly random, and six of the nine traits are read by nothing but the sprite renderer. Insects have a genuinely excellent adaptive layer (the identity map) that is used for exactly one relationship and connects to plant genetics through a single integer. Critters cannot be born, cannot die, and cannot change. The word for what the flora does is drift, not adaptation: a random walk with a speciation tripwire attached.

← Part I §16 — the twelve structural gaps this argues from

2 · Five things that are structurally impossiblenot rare — impossible, at any seed, at any tuning

#ImpossibleBecause
1A trait becoming more common because it works simTick reproduces a uniformly-sampled plant at a flat reproChance; the crowding thin removes a uniformly-sampled plant. Genome never enters either decision. (Part I §11)
2An animal adapting to a plant Palate, role, size and morph are frozen at worldgen. There is no fauna reproduction of any kind. (Part I §9)
3Two species partitioning a resource The only scarcity is tile slots. No light, water, nutrient, or time axis exists to partition.
4Something new entering the world The species list can only split, bounded at 12 daughters. No source term. (Part I §12)
5A disturbance the ecology recovers from No fire, blight, flood, storm or die-off. Succession has no reset, so diversity has no maintenance mechanism.

Note the shape: (1) and (2) are about selection, (3) about niche, (4) about origination, (5) about disturbance. Those four words are the whole ecology. The engine currently implements none of them — and implements inheritance beautifully.

3 · Foundationsfour small changes that turn drift into evolution

Deliberately tiny — and, I think, the highest value-per-line work available in this codebase. None adds an entity, a save field, or a key.

3.1 · Selective mortality — make the thin choose

flora.ts:534 picks a crowding victim at random. Change it to sample k = 3 and remove the worst by some local fitness. That single edit is the difference between a random walk and evolution, because it is the first time a genome ever affects a plant's expected descendants.

fitness(p) = 1 × shadeTerm(p) // §3.3 — tall wins light, short loses it × kinTerm(p) // penalty for being surrounded by near-identical kin × visitTerm(p) // bonus if recently fed on / pollinated

Every term is optional and independently shippable. Even one makes the island's flora start bending toward something.

Design guard. Selection must express as a rate, never an event. A plant that loses does not die visibly of losing; it is simply the one the crowd thins. The peaceful pillar survives intact — this is the same mechanism that already runs, just with an opinion.

3.2 · Make spread govern dispersal distance

spread (0.1–1) currently draws bushier sprites. Let it scale reseedRadius: a high-spread plant throws children further but with a lower per-seed take rate (fewer placement attempts, or a per-tile penalty).

This is the colonization–competition tradeoff, the single most-studied coexistence mechanism in real ecology. Two species can then stably coexist on one habitat: one wins locally, one wins the empty ground. Today, two species on one habitat simply race and one wins. Cost: about ten lines in propagate.

3.3 · Make height govern light

One float per tile: shade = Σ height over the plants on it. A plant's reproduction multiplier reads its own height against the shade above it. Cheapest possible niche axis, and it pays enormously:

  • height becomes load-bearing — a real cost/benefit rather than decoration.
  • Forests stratify: canopy, understory, ground layer, with different winners.
  • The forms that currently do nothing ecologically get a role — Moss (0.05–0.25) and Fungus (0.10–0.45) become specialists of the shaded floor rather than just short things.
  • Trees get an ecological function for the first time. (Part I §5)
  • It couples straight into fire, gaps, and succession later.

3.4 · The learned palate — let critters adapt without being born

Fauna can't evolve because they can't reproduce, and giving ~30 named, den-having, trust-bearing creatures a birth/death cycle is a large and possibly unwanted change. There is a cheaper answer that is also better design: let the palate drift toward what the animal actually eats.

On each completed nibble, nudge the kind's hueCenter a hair toward the genome it just fed on (and glowTaste likewise). Bounded, slow, per-kind.

  • It is learning, not genetics — honest to "nothing is born, nothing dies".
  • It closes the co-adaptation loop: plants bend toward their dispersers (already true), and now dispersers bend toward the plants that persist.
  • It is visible: a kind's palate swatch in the codex shifts over island-days, and the journal can note "its taste has moved".
  • It creates the first runaway in the game — disperser prefers red, spreads red, finds more red, prefers red harder. Which is exactly the kind of thing that should then need a brake (§7).

Roughly twenty lines in fauna.ts, one new persisted field.

4 · Multiple pixel mapsgeneralize the identity map into a tag space

The IdMap is the best mechanic in the codebase (Part I §7): a 7×7 grid of 7 states with a reward economy that produces specialists, generalists, and camouflage from one function. It is used for exactly one relationship — insect sensor against flower appearance — and it is not even connected to the plant's real colour.

The move: treat the idmap as a general channel primitive and run several. Same grid, same economy, different meaning. The interesting part is not any single channel; it is the leakage between them.

Trait conduits become physical

The food-web study left this as the open fork. If a substrate carries the eaten plant's signature into the soil map, and germinating plants read the soil map, colour genuinely travels the island. You can watch a hue walk from a meadow to a shore over island-days, carried in bellies. Cause and effect you can trace — the exact thing the Scavenger's Reign study argues makes an ecology feel alive.

Place stops being a boolean

Today a tile either is or isn't your habitat. With a soil map, ground has history — it has been a fungus patch, then a burn, then meadow — and germination reads that history. Islands develop a past you can read from the dirt.

Mimicry without cruelty

A harmless species that copies a costly one's warning map free-rides. A flower that copies a different flower's appearance map borrows its pollinators. Both are real biology, both are gentle, and both are pure emergence from the existing match function.

5 · The missing trophic levelcritters that eat swarms

Almost free, because the top of the food chain is currently a constant: WORLD_PREDATION = 0.6 (game/swarms.ts:61), described in its own comment as "standing in for generic insectivores." Meanwhile one to two bird flocks fly around the island eating nothing at all. (Part I §14)

Add one role: insectivore. Its palate reads sensor maps instead of plant genomes — the same appetite shape on a different tag space. applyPredation already takes a pressure scalar; make that scalar local and sourced rather than global and constant.

What it produces, in order of how surprising it is

  1. Predation becomes geography. Safe bays and dangerous meadows. Swarms are thinned where insectivores actually are.
  2. A frustrated optimization. Right now a cloud has one goal: match the flower. Add a predator with a search image and the cloud has two goals in tension — match the flower to feed, don't match it where you'll be seen. Frustrated optimizations are where persistent diversity comes from in real systems, and they never settle. This is the single best answer to "how do I get surprised by adaptiveness."
  3. Local divergence for free. Clouds in bird country drift away from clouds in safe country while working the same flower. Same species, two shapes, one island. divergeSwarm already exists to bud the cousin.
  4. The predator adapts back. Give the insectivore a search-image map that drifts toward what it has caught, and you have a two-sided arms race in a system where nothing dies.
  5. A three-level colour conduit. Flower colour → insect colour → predator search image. Change the flowers and the birds' preference follows, two links downstream, over island-days.
The peaceful contract holds

applyPredation never kills discretely — it lowers a float. An insectivore is an animal that makes clouds thinner near it, visible as a cloud that keeps its distance and stays drab. No corpse, no chase, no fear. The existing Drives.fear slot stays deferred; predation here is a pressure on populations, not an event in a life.

The chassis is already there. life/birds.ts has flocks that fly, settle, perch and flush. Give a flock a sensor map and a feeding radius and the system is done.

And the symmetric move: a frugivore/nectarivore critter whose palate reads the flower's appearance map rather than the plant's genome — so pollinator-plant and disperser-plant relationships stop living in separate alphabets.

6 · The Hearta source term for the world

The species list is closed. Islands can split lineages twelve times and then they are done, forever. There is nothing in Wonder that makes new kinds of thing, and that is the deepest reason a long session eventually settles.

What it is

A rare worldgen feature alongside Crater and Pocket: a cave mouth in the rock, faintly lit, breathing on a slow cycle. Perhaps one island in six. Visible from a distance at night.

What it emits

Every island-day or so, a Mote of the Unformed: an entity whose only genome is a random idmap, with no form, habitat, role or species, and an economy deliberately set to losing — high upkeep, near-zero income. A Mote is dying from the moment it appears. It has a few island-hours to find something to be.

How it becomes something

The Mote wanders and samples. Each tick it scores its map against every channel in reach. Reward accumulates per channel. When one channel's accumulated reward crosses a threshold, the Mote commits — see the diagram. Commitment is speciation from nothing. The new kind enters the ordinary species list, gets a name in the codex voice, and lives under all the ordinary caps.

Why this is the surprise engine

Because what a Mote becomes is determined by what the island had room for. Release one into a saturated meadow and it starves and returns. Release one into a habitat with an unworked flower, and it becomes the pollinator that flower never had. Release one on burnt ground and it becomes a fire-follower. You cannot predict the output, and the output is always an answer to a question the island was actually asking. That is a fundamentally different kind of surprise from "the dice rolled a purple one."

The player verb it creates

Motes can be carried. Pick one up in the pouch and it becomes the most interesting item in the game, because where you release it decides what it is. Release it in the marsh and it becomes a reed; carry it to the shore and it becomes something that lives in the shallows. A player who understands the system can aim the world's creativity without ever setting a value — exactly the "tilt the dice, never set them" line the ecosystem vision drew for the bench, arrived at from the other direction.

Balance and the peaceful pillar

  • Emission is bounded and slow; commitment requires a genuine reward threshold, so most Motes fade.
  • Make the emission rate read the island's unfilled niche volume: a rich island's Heart is quiet, a barren one's is prolific. The source term self-balances, and a flat seed (like seed 42) is no longer a dud — it is a place where the Heart is busy.
  • Fading is not death: a Mote that fails returns to the Heart, and the Heart remembers. Its future output drifts toward everything that has ever come back to it. An island's Heart becomes a slowly-accumulating memory of its own failures — and ghost lineages can resurface much later.

The prior-art doc's strongest finding is unused: making carrying capacity vary periodically enlarges the zone in which species coexist (Swailem & Täuber, Phys. Rev. E 107, 2023). Wonder has day/night, tide, rain, bloom-day and aurora — five periodic signals (Part I §2) — and not one of them touches carrying capacity. Wiring rain or season to per-tile nutrient (or even just to maxPlants) is a one-line change with a theoretically-grounded stabilizing effect.

And the rule that keeps all of it peaceful: every driver above expresses as a change in rate, never as an event in an individual's life. Nothing starves, nothing is hunted, nothing suffers. Populations rise and fall.

8 · Weird things to put in the worldthe surprise budget, by delight per unit of work

Wind

One vector per island, rolled at worldgen. Biases the tile choice in propagate and pollinateSpread. Windward and leeward floras diverge; seeds pile against ridges; the island develops an orientation. Perhaps ten lines, and it makes every map read differently.

The seed bank

A dying plant leaves a dormant substrate carrying its full genome. Disturbance — fire, flood, a player's hoe — germinates it. Nothing is ever truly lost; the island remembers its dead. Tilling old ground and finding a lineage that vanished twenty island-days ago is one of the best possible moments this game could have.

Fire

The missing disturbance, and the classic diversity-maintenance mechanism. Lightning on a storm night, on a dry island, burns a swath. It enriches the soil map rather than sterilizing it. High-glow forms burn hot (glow reads as volatile oils — a real and lovely reason the psychedelic lineages are dangerous to stand in). Fire-followers germinate only on burnt soil. Peaceful, because it is weather.

Symbiogenesis

Two species that co-occur densely and match hues for long enough fuse into a composite kind inheriting both parents' habitats. Mechanically cross() across a species boundary plus a habitat union. Produces genuinely astonishing organisms — a moss-coral that lives on rock and in the shallows — and it is real biology. Reserve it heavily: once or twice per long-lived island.

Epiphytes — habitat as a form

Let a species' habitat be "on a Tree" rather than "on Forest floor". This doubles the niche space with no new tile types, and it finally gives trees an ecological role. Vines and mosses want this badly; they already read as things that should grow on something.

The midden

Critter dens accumulate substrate over island-days and become nutrient hotspots with their own distinctive flora. Dens stop being a coordinate and become a place you can find by looking at the plants. The vision doc's "little factories" arrives without a crafting system.

The intertidal guild

Tide already exists and is cosmetic. Plants that can only reproduce at low tide, or only germinate on ground the tide has just uncovered, create a temporal niche and a reason to be on the shore at a particular hour.

Ghost pollinators

When a cloud's host species goes extinct it keeps its sensor map — a specialist with nothing left to specialize on. It either finds a mimic, or fades. Watching a perfectly-adapted thing become obsolete is a kind of story this game has no other way to tell.

Chorus and sensory exploitation

Insect clouds converge on a shared signal channel. A flower whose appearance map drifts toward that signal gets visits it never earned. Deception without menace — the "mutualism's white lie" the Scavenger's Reign study already sketched.

9 · How they should competethree channels, and one rule

The rule that makes all three safe is the same: competition lowers a rate; it never triggers an event.

#ChannelWhat it produces
1SpaceExists already, but is adjudicated by a coin flip. Make the thin selective (§3.1). Nothing new is needed; the existing brake simply acquires an opinion.
2LightVertical, one float per tile (§3.3). The axis that produces structure: layers, understories, gaps, succession — and the one that makes the most currently-inert forms suddenly make sense.
3AttentionPollinator choice (§7). The axis that produces display: colour, petal count, scent. The only competition that gets more beautiful the fiercer it gets — the most on-brand of the three.

What to explicitly not add: interference competition (plants attacking plants), territoriality, exclusion. Wonder's whole thesis is that a peaceful world can still be dynamic. Everything above achieves dynamism through differential rates, which is both gentler and — per the prior-art doc — closer to how real ecologies actually hold together.

10 · Instrumentationyou cannot tune what you cannot see

The World-Lab was built to make food chains legible and currently plots only population counts. Four readouts, in order of value:

  1. Trait distributions over time. A hue histogram per species, sampled on the census cadence. The single most revealing chart the game could have — and the sampling infrastructure already exists (CensusLog.sample takes a map; it just takes the wrong map). Right now genome drift, the thing the entire flora layer is about, is invisible in aggregate. (Part I §10)
  2. A fitness readout, the moment a fitness function exists: per-species mean fitness, and a trait-vs-fitness scatter. This is how you'd know whether §3.1 actually did anything.
  3. The lineage tree. PlantSpecies.parent and .bornTick are recorded and never drawn. A dendrogram of an island's ✧ daughters is three hours of work and it is the island's autobiography.
  4. A niche-space map. Plot every species in (habitat × hue × height) and show the empty regions. Both a design instrument and, later, the Heart's targeting display — it shows you what the island is about to invent.

11 · Recommended build ordereach phase independently shippable

Phase 0 — make the existing genome matter

Small, and where I'd start.

  • selective crowding thin (§3.1)
  • height → shade, one float per tile (§3.3)
  • spread → dispersal distance with a take-rate cost (§3.2)
  • learned palate (§3.4)
  • trait-distribution charts (§10.1) — so you can see whether any of it worked

Four small mechanics and one chart. At the end, Wonder has evolution instead of drift, two genome traits stop being decoration, and the first co-adaptive loop closes. Nothing new is on screen.

Phase 1 — the soil map

Generalize IdMap to a second channel (§4).

Tiles accumulate a signature from what dies and is dropped there; germination reads it. This is the substrate that fire, the seed bank, succession and trait-conduits all stand on.

Phase 2 — the insectivore

One role, one search-image map, birds as the chassis (§5).

Predation becomes local, sourced and adaptive; camouflage becomes a real game with two players; local divergence appears without any new species machinery.

Phase 3 — the Heart

The source term (§6).

Open-ended origination, and the best player verb in the design space.

Phase 4 — disturbance and strangeness

Fire, blight (already specced), symbiogenesis, epiphytes, wind, the midden, the intertidal guild.

By this point every one of them lands into a system with selection, niche and origination already running, so each addition compounds rather than sitting beside the others.

12 · Open questionsfor Blaine

  1. Fauna: learned palate, or real reproduction? §3.4 gets co-adaptation without birth or death, and preserves the named-friend feel of critters. A true birth/death cycle would be more powerful and would cost the trust and companion systems their stability. My strong lean is learned palate — but it's your call whether critters should ever be populations.
  2. How many idmap channels before it stops being legible? Three (appearance, scent, soil) feels like the ceiling for a player meant to read the system by watching. Warning and song are wonderful and might be one layer too many.
  3. The Heart: one per island, or one per world? A single Heart shared across all islands — its memory accumulating across every island you've ever visited — is a much stranger and possibly better idea than one per island.
  4. Does the Heart emit into the player's pouch, or only into the world? The carry-and-release verb (§6) is the most player-facing idea in this document, and the one most likely to break the "the world doesn't need you" pillar.
  5. Fire. Is a burn compatible with peaceful, or does watching a stand of plants you loved go up cross the line? My read: it's compatible, and it's the most important missing mechanic. But it's the one I'd most want you to veto if it isn't.
